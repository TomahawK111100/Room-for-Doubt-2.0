\section{Independent CIFAR-10.1 evaluation}
\label{sec:confirmation-s4}
We trained three ResNet50 bases on CIFAR-10N Worse (seeds 42--44, shared
noisy train and validation split). The saved training snapshots, heads
and validation-selected policies were frozen before evaluation on all
2,000 images of CIFAR-10.1 v6~\citep{recht2019imagenet}. We compared the base, the Early-5 residual
head, a direct soft-target head with the same training targets and budget,
and the mean of five early snapshots. The primary contrast was residual
Early-5 versus its base. The protocol required a mean gain of at least
0.50 percentage points with a positive lower bound of a paired 95\%
interval, and a mean teacher gap of at most 2 points. Resampling preserved
class counts and paired image IDs across all three fitted bases.

Early-5 residual changed accuracy by $+0.35$, $-1.05$ and $+0.50$ points
across bases (mean $-0.07$, between-base SD $0.85$, conditional paired
95\% interval $[-0.53, 0.42]$). The mean teacher gap was 1.73 points
(interval $[1.07, 2.40]$). Latency was $1.008\times$ the base and
$0.230\times$ the teacher, within the prespecified limits of $1.10\times$
and $0.50\times$, respectively. The run therefore met the latency
criterion and fell short of the primary accuracy criterion. Direct
distillation gained 0.57 points over the base on average in a secondary
comparison (nominal conditional paired interval $[0.02, 1.13]$ points).
Relative to the base, the residual head corrected 105 and introduced 109 errors across the three sets of predictions. Direct distillation corrected 165 and introduced 131. These counts sum over seed--image pairs on the same test set.
The intervals quantify variation across test images
conditional on the fitted bases.
