\section{Results}
\paragraph{Accuracy on independently fitted bases.}
Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} compares policies selected on noisy validation; Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy_gain} shows gains relative to each base. Early-5 with base features raises CIFAR-100N accuracy from 70.12\% to 71.08\% (base 42), 69.23\% to 70.28\% (base 43), and 69.57\% to 70.09\% (base 44). On CIFAR-10N Worse, accuracy rises from 88.84\% to 90.16\% (base 42), 89.63\% to 91.28\% (base 43), and 88.49\% to 90.39\% (base 44). Gains are positive for the head mean on all six bases. However, the nominal paired interval for CIFAR-100N base 44, head 43 is $[-0.19,+0.82]$ points relative to its base, and includes zero (Appendix~\ref{app:intervals}). Positive intervals against same-view TTA need not imply positive intervals against the globally selected TTA policy.

Averaging heads within each base and then independent bases equally, CIFAR-100N accuracy changes from 69.64\% to 70.48\%, a mean gain of 0.84 points (between-base sample SD 0.28; $n=3$ bases). CIFAR-10N Worse changes from 88.99\% to 90.61\%, a gain of 1.62 points (sample SD 0.29; $n=3$ bases). Mean gains over validation-selected uniform TTA are 0.82 points on CIFAR-100N and 1.07 points on CIFAR-10N. These small replication counts do not establish a universal effect or a precise population-level gain.

\paragraph{Target construction with matched heads.}
With base features, static-margin regression obtains 70.35\%, 69.27\%, and 69.57\% for CIFAR-100N bases 42/43/44, and 89.19\%, 90.53\%, and 88.82\% for CIFAR-10N Worse bases 42/43/44. Early-5 is higher in each case with the same head architecture and policy-search procedure; the History-50 mean is also lower than Early-5 on each base. The difference is therefore not explained solely by adding a trainable scalar head. It does not distinguish temporal information from the different augmentation and measurement conditions used to construct the targets. These are comparisons of the complete validation-selected policies; their selected temperatures and sometimes view budgets differ. Direct paired target comparisons now cover all six bases (Appendix~\ref{app:followup_controls}).

\paragraph{Augmentation-matched static control.}
On all three bases of each dataset, a follow-up static target averages five new deterministic draws from the actual training augmentation distribution at the fixed selected checkpoint. It uses the same frozen base features, train-only PCA, head architecture, three head seeds and 25-epoch budget. Nine base-feature heads per base are reused by the augmentation stage; only three augmented-static heads are fitted. The CIFAR-10N base-42 study was first rebuilt on its verified original checkpoint, reproducing the old policies and accuracies. Policies are selected on noisy validation before test evaluation. This matches the augmentation distribution, not the unrecorded original RNG draws or changing online weights.

For CIFAR-100N bases 42/43/44, augmented-static accuracy is 70.32\%, 69.27\%, and 69.57\%, versus Early-5's 71.08\%, 70.28\%, and 70.09\%. The per-base mean gaps are 0.76, 1.01, and 0.52 points, averaging 0.76 points over three independent bases (sample SD 0.24). For CIFAR-10N bases 42/43/44, augmented-static accuracy is 89.28\%, 90.61\%, and 88.91\%, versus Early-5's 90.16\%, 91.28\%, and 90.39\%. Gaps are 0.88, 0.67, and 1.48 points, averaging 1.01 points (sample SD 0.42; three bases). Seven of nine CIFAR-100N nominal paired head-level accuracy intervals are strictly positive: all six on bases 42/43, and one on base 44; the other two base-44 intervals include zero. All nine CIFAR-10N intervals are positive. Original-view AUROC difference intervals are positive for all eighteen heads. Each policy can choose a different view budget: the new CIFAR-100N static controls choose ten views, while Early-5 chooses five. These conditional results strengthen the evidence beyond the original static target, but do not isolate time from weight evolution, checkpoint timing or the CE/GCE schedule.

\paragraph{Early summaries and feature sources.}
The initial CIFAR-100N seed-42 window study is reported in Table~\ref{tab:windows} and Figure~\ref{fig:history_windows}. The first ten epochs give the highest observed test accuracy in that sweep, while the last five epochs and the full-history mean are weaker. This sweep is exploratory and does not select an optimal window using test accuracy. The primary Early-5 target also coincides with the cross-entropy warmup; later epochs use a different loss. Thus the window effect combines learning time and the loss schedule.

For Early-5, replacing ResNet18 features with selected-base features changes accuracy from 70.41\% to 71.08\% on CIFAR-100N seed 42, from 69.32\% to 70.28\% on seed 43, and from 90.01\% to 90.16\% on CIFAR-10N seed 42. The full six-base comparison, including the new ResNet18 controls, appears in Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}. Both encoders are fixed during head training, but their pretraining and task adaptation differ. This comparison supports using the selected base representation under this recipe; it does not isolate encoder architecture from adaptation.

\paragraph{Error ranking and calibration.}
Early-5 with base features increases original-view error AUROC on all six bases. For CIFAR-100N bases 42/43/44, MSP-to-Early-5 values are 0.8124 to 0.8307, 0.8222 to 0.8326, and 0.8281 to 0.8325. For CIFAR-10N Worse bases 42/43/44 they are 0.8764 to 0.9064, 0.8758 to 0.8976, and 0.8571 to 0.9156. These rankings are separate from the gains in aggregated prediction accuracy.

CIFAR-10N calibration is metric- and base-dependent: ECE changes from 0.2847 to 0.2756 on base 42, but from 0.2518 to 0.2542 on base 43 and 0.2638 to 0.2672 on base 44. NLL improves on all three (0.6203 to 0.5903; 0.5591 to 0.5359; 0.6009 to 0.5781). On all three CIFAR-100N bases, Early-5 worsens both ECE and NLL relative to the original-view base. Better accuracy or error ranking therefore does not imply improved probability calibration. Full results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}.

\paragraph{Ensembles and computational cost.}
A fixed original-view average of CIFAR-100N bases 42 and 43 reaches 73.01\%, exceeding either base with Early-5 weighting. This is the previously completed two-base ensemble; no new three-base ensemble is reported. Its nominal paired gain over base 42 is 2.89 points, with interval $[2.32,3.50]$. The comparison uses a different inference budget: the ensemble evaluates two bases once each, whereas the selected Early-5 policies evaluate one base on five or ten views depending on base and head. Globally selected uniform TTA uses ten views on five bases, but one view on CIFAR-100N base 44. There is therefore no demonstrated latency or forward-pass advantage over this two-base ensemble. Relative to uniform TTA at the same number of views, base-feature reuse avoids a second image encoder but adds PCA and the head.

Training cost includes the 50-epoch base run, trajectory recording, feature extraction, PCA, and head fitting. The experiments fit multiple controls and head seeds, so campaign wall time is not the cost of fitting a single deployment head. Storage for 45,000 float32 margins over 50 epochs alone is 9 MB in decimal units; cached features, weights, and predictions add further storage. The head contains $139{,}777+32C$ trainable parameters for $C$ classes. Training-overhead measurements remain needed to quantify the full tradeoff. A completed T4 inference microbenchmark is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:runtime}: for one image and five views on CIFAR-100N base 44, medians including CPU preprocessing and transfer are 20.25 ms for uniform TTA and 20.36 ms for Early-5. For ten views on CIFAR-10N base 43 they are 40.93 and 41.01 ms. Corresponding one-view base measurements are 7.59 and 7.52 ms. Thus the observed incremental head cost is small relative to matched-view TTA, while multi-view inference remains more expensive than one base pass. One session and fixed timing order do not establish statistical equivalence of these close medians or an advantage over an ensemble.
